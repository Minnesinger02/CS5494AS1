The final FVSBN samples are recognisably digit-shaped but fragmented: strokes break and several digits stay ambiguous, which matches its test NLL of 75.7 nats/image (0.139 bits/pixel) and the fact that a linear conditional can only add up evidence from earlier pixels. MADE produces clearly cleaner 28 x 28 samples with continuous strokes and legible 0-9 shapes, consistent with its lower test NLL of 68.6 nats/image (0.126 bits/pixel); the visual comparison is qualitative, not a blinded human evaluation. The 10,000-image ablation keeps the same ordering (120.2 versus 80.2 nats/image), so the advantage is not an artefact of the full training set. GAN samples cover all ten digit classes and the independent MNIST classifier scores them at mean confidence 0.844 against 0.939 for real digits, with predicted-class entropy 2.277 out of a possible 2.303 and a feature-Frechet distance of 7.3 against a real-data reference of 0. This argues against both class collapse and blank collapse in this run, although individual samples are noisy and some 3, 5 and 8 shapes remain malformed. Generator and discriminator losses stay finite and balanced (about 0.93 and 1.13 at the end); an earlier run with equal learning rates drifted into a dominant discriminator (generator BCE rising from 1.15 to 1.62) and produced a much worse feature distance, so it is reported only as an ablation. Training-stage grids show the expected transition from unstructured output to recognisable strokes. All three models remain compact baselines rather than high-fidelity digit generators.
